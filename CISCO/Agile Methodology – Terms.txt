Agile Methodology – Terms
1. Agile
- Software is developed in small parts instead of building everything at once.
- Changes can be made based on customer feedback.
- Working software is delivered frequently.

2. Scrum
- Scrum is a framework used to manage work using Agile principles.
- The team divides work into short periods called Sprints.
- The team plans, develops, reviews, and improves the work regularly.

3. Sprint
- A Sprint is a fixed period, usually 1–4 weeks.
- The team works on selected tasks during this period.
- At the end, the team aims to deliver a working product improvement.

4. Iteration
- Iteration means repeating the development process to improve the software.
- The team builds a version and checks how it works.
- The next cycle improves or adds features to the existing version.

5. User Story
- A user story describes a feature from the user's point of view.
- It explains what the user wants and why.

6. Epic
- An epic is a large feature that needs to be divided into smaller tasks or user stories.
- It may take several Sprints to complete.
- Example: An online shopping system with login, cart, and payment features.

7. Story Point
- Story points estimate how difficult a task is.
- The team considers complexity, effort, and uncertainty.
- More story points generally mean the task is more challenging compared with other tasks.

8. Acceptance Criteria
- Acceptance criteria are conditions a feature must satisfy to be accepted.
- They explain how the feature should behave.
- Example: Login should succeed with valid credentials and reject invalid credentials.


Scrum Roles – Responsibilities
1. Product Owner (PO)
- Decides which features are most important.
- Maintains and prioritizes the Product Backlog.
- Explains requirements to the team.

2. Scrum Master (SM)
- Helps the team follow Scrum.
- Helps resolve obstacles affecting progress.
- Ensures Scrum events are useful and effective.

3. Developers
- Develop, test, and maintain the software.
- Decide how to complete the selected work.
- Work together to deliver a working product increment.

4. Stakeholders
- Include customers, managers, and others interested in the product.
- Share their requirements and expectations.
- Give feedback on the product.


Scrum Events and Backlogs
1. Sprint Planning
- The team(PO,SM,Developers) decides what work to do in the Sprint.
- They set a Sprint Goal.
- They plan how to complete the selected work.

2. Daily Scrum
- A short, 15-minute daily meeting for Developers.
- The team checks progress toward the Sprint Goal.
- They adjust their plan and identify problems.

3. Sprint Review
- The team shows the completed work.
- Stakeholders provide feedback.
- The team discusses what could be done next.

4. Sprint Retrospective
- The team discusses how the Sprint went.
- They identify what went well and what went wrong.
- They decide how to improve in the next Sprint.

5. Sprint Backlog
- Contains the work selected for the current Sprint.
- Includes the Sprint Goal and the plan to achieve it.
- Developers update the plan as work progresses.

6. Sprint Goal
- A Sprint Goal is the main objective the team wants to achieve during a Sprint.

7. Definition of Done (DoD)
- Definition of Done is a common set of conditions that work must satisfy to be considered complete.
- It ensures the feature is properly developed, tested, and meets the team's quality standards.
- Example: The login feature is coded, tested, reviewed, and has no known critical bugs.
